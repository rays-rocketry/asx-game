Candidates, ranked

Ranked by how likely the catalyst is to land inside the game window, not by how big the move could be. A 79% event beats a 15% event even when the 15% one pays more. Odds come from published base rates — sources at the bottom.

Rule the game imposes on you: it ends 22 October. Anything scheduled after that date is worthless here, however good it looks. That single filter removed several of the strongest ideas — November trial results, the 31 October quarterly reports.
1FleetPartnersFPR Takeover~75% odds

Vehicle leasing company, valued at about $854 million, with three buyers bidding against each other: Element Fleet at up to $4.00 a share, SG Fleet also at $4.00 (raised from $3.60, which the board rejected), and Japan's ORIX at $3.80. A fourth, Smartgroup, has walked away.

Trigger: any morning — the board has refused to give anyone exclusivity, so bidding stays open

Risk: all three could walk — none of the offers is binding yet, and one bidder already has. The share price has jumped on the news, so you are buying the second half of the move. Two bidders are level at $4.00, so the next step up may be small.

2The ASX 200 reshuffle4 SEP Forced buying~70% odds

Four times a year the list of Australia's biggest 200 companies is updated, and index funds are obliged to buy whatever is added — regardless of price. September is the larger half-yearly review: roughly $2.5 billion of forced trading.

Announced Friday 4 September · takes effect Friday 18 September

Risk: research shows the gain lands mostly on announcement day and then partly reverses after the change takes effect. Buy on the 4th, sell into the 18th. Holding past that gives some back. The effect is also smaller than it used to be.

3EQT HoldingsEQT Takeover~70% odds

A live bidding war. TPG offered $24.55 a share, then BGH Capital countered at $24.75 three days later. The shares had their best single day in 41 years on the first offer.

Trigger: days to weeks — neither bidder has been let in to inspect the books yet, and the board has made no recommendation

Risk: the first bid delivered most of the gain and the counter-bid added only 20c. Most of the value is already in the price. Both offers are non-binding, and TPG's depends on the board recommending it unanimously — which has not happened.

4Reliance WorldwideRWC Takeover~65% odds

Plumbing fittings maker. Brookfield has already raised its offer once, to $4.1 billion — which usually means the buyer is serious and the board pushed back successfully.

Trigger: weeks — the board formally recommending it to shareholders

Risk: a bigger, slower deal. Regulatory approval on a company this size can run past 22 October, so the gain may land after your game has finished.

5AustalASB Takeover~50% odds

Korean group Hanwha offered US$1.05–1.20 billion for Austal's American shipyard at Mobile, Alabama, announced 10 August; the shares jumped 17%. The Australian business is not included. Hanwha was given four weeks to inspect the books and talk to the US Navy.

Deadline: around 7 September — a real date in the diary

Risk: genuinely two-sided. A firm offer sends it up; walking away sends it down hard. This is the most binary item on the list — treat it as a small position, not a big one.

6Caravel MineralsCVV Mining study~45% odds

A copper company publishing its final engineering and costing study in September — the document that says whether the mine is worth building and at what return. It has already lifted its measured ore reserve to 597 million tonnes.

Due: September · plus a possible copper supply deal with an Adani subsidiary

Risk: studies slip. This one has already been delayed once for environmental and water work. A weak return number sends the shares down, not up.

7Aura EnergyAEE Build decision~40% odds

The board decides whether to actually build its uranium mine. Published economics show a strong projected return.

Targeted: by 30 September

Risk: a decision to build without the funding attached usually disappoints. Check whether the money is in place, not just the decision.

8Vertex MineralsVTX New equipment~35% odds

A small gold miner switching to bigger, more efficient mining machinery — less waste rock, higher grade ore to the mill. It rose 46% in one week in August on the very first test blast.

New rig arrives mid-September · full switchover through October

Risk: the easy money may already be made — the 46% happened before you got here. Small companies like this fall as fast as they rise.

Why Megaport is not on this list. Megaport was held by 7 of the 14 winning portfolios I analysed — by far the most common winning stock. It is deliberately excluded, because that is last game's winner. Those winners bought it before it ran; copying them now means buying after. Picking a stock because it already went up is the single most common mistake in this game.

Decision diary

A team finishing at $52,000 with a clear record of why usually beats a team that lucked into $70,000 with no notes. Write the reason before you know the outcome — that is the whole point.

Ask for help

Stuck on a word or an idea? Ask here. It explains things — it will not tell you what to buy, because working that out is the point.

Good questions: what does this word mean · why would that make a share price move · what should we watch for on 4 September · explain the risk on candidate 5.
